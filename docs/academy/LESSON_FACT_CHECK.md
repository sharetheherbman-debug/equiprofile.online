# EquiProfile Academy Lesson Fact Check

## Scope and method

This record supports the production review of the 105 Academy lessons. It is not a veterinary protocol or an accreditation claim. Higher-risk claims are checked against current, authoritative UK welfare, government, veterinary, or governing-body material. Lessons retain the boundary that owners should contact an appropriate veterinary professional for diagnosis, treatment, or urgent advice.

## Curriculum-wide factual and safety rules

The lesson source must not claim accreditation by BHS, Pony Club, British Equestrian, FEI, RCVS, BEVA, or any other external organisation. A source informs review; it does not grant endorsement or accreditation.

Where a subject can vary by individual horse, country, competition, medication, season, disease status, or current regulation, the lesson must teach safe observation and decision-making, then direct the learner to a veterinarian, farrier, qualified professional, organiser, insurer, transporter, or current official rule as appropriate.

> **Emergency boundary:** Academy lessons may teach preparation, observation, and safe first-response priorities. They must not replace urgent veterinary assessment, prescribe medication, give treatment dosages, or encourage a learner to delay professional help.

## High-risk subject register

| Topic group                                                       | Lesson-review control                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First response, colic, laminitis, wounds, lameness, vital signs   | Confirm urgent-referral wording; avoid diagnosis and treatment prescriptions; retain a direct veterinary escalation route.  |
| Vaccination, parasite control, dentistry, farriery and medication | Teach purpose, records, and professional planning; avoid static schedules or prescribing.                                   |
| Nutrition, supplements, youngstock and senior horses              | Teach condition-led observation and forage-first principles; direct individual diet changes to an appropriate professional. |
| Biosecurity and infectious disease                                | Keep isolation, hygiene, equipment separation, visitor/arrival planning, and disease-alert/veterinary escalation prominent. |
| Transport, insurance, ownership and competition                   | Avoid claiming fixed legal or competition rules; direct learners to current official bodies and insurers.                   |
| Safeguarding, risk and welfare                                    | Emphasise reporting routes, supervision, consent, and escalation rather than informal investigation or judgement.           |
